Project Description
Background

A 2021 study found that 63.0% of surveyed older adults stated driving was their primary mode of

community mobility and transportation (Marfeo et al., 2021). Nationally, approximately 57 million
individuals live with disabilities, and over 40% rely on others for transportation due to personal and
environmental barriers that prevent them from driving (Dicianno et al., 2021). However, only 9.2%
reported receiving rehabilitation services focused on driving or transportation-related goals in the past
year (Marfeo et al., 2021). These barriers—including limited access to adapted vehicles, decreased
driving confidence, and lack of knowledge about driving resources—can lead to social isolation, reduced
access to healthcare, and diminished quality of life (Park et al., 2023). Driving and community mobility
are among the most important meaningful occupations individuals can engage in, facilitating performance
in other areas of life including leisure, work, and social participation (Stav, 2012).

 As older adults age, barriers to community mobility can be addressed through appropriate

specialized driving rehabilitation services. Despite this service option, the Co-PI is currently the only
Driving Rehabilitation Professional and NYS Certified Driving Instructor on Long Island who has a
healthcare background and education, creating an immense opportunity for the study team to meet
regional driving rehabilitation needs. Traditional driving rehabilitation services involve comprehensive
clinical evaluations of vision, cognition, and physical/motor skills, and on the road assessments and
training. However, evidence suggests that naturalistic driving methods deploying mobile health
technology may be engaging and show greater representativeness of performance than traditional driving
assessments for older adults (Babulal et al., 2016; Bayat et al., 2021; Cammarata, 2024).

In fact, in-situ behavioral data collection via sensors and passenger real-time inputs may have
great clinical value and provide a richer context of driving behaviors to evaluate older adults’ driving
safety and performance. When combined with self-reports and passenger rated performance, sensor-based
application will provide a more comprehensive picture of driving rehabilitation intervention needs in a
more naturalistic experience. Importantly, including passengers—often caregivers or family members—
integrates a novel opportunity with observing  the driver’s behaviors, safety, and concerns following the
prompted in-app system that is categorized by driving skills. Mobile health interventions offer
opportunities to individualize targeted training programs and deliver goal-specific driving supports in situ
as an extension of 1:1 clinical rehabilitation sessions. The smartphone presents a ubiquitous vehicle to
collect objective sensor data and subjective performance (report) data and, importantly, presents the
potential for a performance feedback system targeted to the individual drivers.

The proposed project recognizes the variability in driver needs and supports dynamic
customization of its interventions based on current functional abilities. Via the proposed mobile health
methods, educational content is deployed in a scaffolded manner as the driver demonstrates consistency
and safety with skills outside of clinical sessions. This approach enables just-in-time interventions based
on caregiver data inputs, clinician alerts, or goal-based strategies, and ultimately could enhance validity of
performance data that supports individualized, continuous care and aligns with both the client’s goals and
the rehabilitation plan. Together, caregiver support and the mobile health approach bridges key elements
to create the foundation for advancing innovative practice. The proposed study utilizes technology to
extend traditional rehabilitation services and promote an individualized approach that can also serve as a
tool for caregivers, family members, and driver rehabilitation professionals. The overall goals of this
project are to elevate the standards of traditional assessments by developing actionable tools and enhance
driving safety during real-world driving experiences.

Specific Aims
Driving and community mobility are critical for older adults’ independence, health access, and quality of
life. However, older adults and individuals with disabilities face personal and environmental barriers to
safe driving, and very few receive rehabilitation services due to the lack of services or availability. There
is a critical need for accessible, interdisciplinary driver rehabilitation programs and data-driven tools to
guide clinical decisions. This project aims to fill this gap and test a novel, mobile health app during

real-world experiences while integrating clinical evaluations and passenger feedback to support clinical
decision-making.
Aim 1: Evaluate the validity of the mobile-health app with assessing the domains across Michon’s driving
behavior levels by comparing them to established cognitive, visual, and motor assessments.
App-based measures will correlate with standard clinical tests, demonstrating concurrent validity.
Aim 2: Evaluate the feasibility and validity of a mobile health app platform for collecting real-world data
on driver behavior and self-reported fitness to drive among older adults.
Aim 3: Explore the relationship between clinical evaluation results and real-world driver feedback
collected via the smartphone-based platform.This aim will examine the alignment of clinical assessments
with feedback and trip patterns reported by drivers and caregivers. Insights from this comparison will help
guide future, personalized approaches to driver rehabilitation and safety training.

Methodical Approach and Timeline

The investigators have laid the groundwork for this study to be completed. Community
stakeholders and the target population have been identified as part of a projected,  August 2025, pilot
program, essential for tailoring the mobile health app. Educational materials and workshops have been
developed to highlight the benefits of driver rehabilitation, screening procedures, and the safety impact of
these services. These tools will also support program expansion beyond the SBM network. Additionally,
following the Co-PI’s invited presentation at the SBM Town Hall, Drs. Verghese and Ambrose have both
committed to participating in this study by supporting the developed provider referral and screening
process and pre-screening participants for this project. These steps have positioned the PI’s team to
expand on intervention and clinical assessment tools to develop an effective Driver Rehabilitation
Program.

Study Eligibility. Participants will be recruited through a physician referral process developed by

the Co-PI team and with Drs. Verghese and Ambrose. Eligible participants include fully-licensed adult
drivers (Class D–NYS) accompanied by an adult passenger who also holds a valid license. Both must own
smartphones capable of running the study platform.

Smartphone-based assessments will capture driving behavior in participants’ natural

environments, thus minimizing recall bias and enhancing ecological validity. To address Aim 1, drivers
and caregivers will use the platform to provide brief post-trip reflections: drivers will rate performance
and confidence, and passengers will log safety behaviors and comfort levels using a checklist generated
from that week’s intervention focus. Study staff will support users and establish personalized prompts
based on participant goals and in-clinic performance. We will collect basic in-car or pre-operational skills
and also deliver objective, real time feedback on driver safety following Michon’s Hierarchical Model of
Driving Behavior which categorizes driving skills into 3 levels (strategic, tactical, and operational).

We will explore correlations between clinical assessment scores and smartphone-derived data.

This includes comparing objective clinical findings (vision, cognition, motor) with driver self-reports and
passenger safety observations to address Aim 2. These analyses will generate early evidence for aligning
clinical indicators with real-world behavior, strengthening the case for scalability, personalized driving
rehabilitation interventions, and informing future funding proposals. Finally, we will analyze the
relationships between clinical findings, self-reported driver performance, and caregiver safety
observations to address Aim 3. The resulting data will help establish early evidence linking in-clinic
assessments with real-world behaviors. These findings will inform future program development, support
scalability, and contribute to personalized driving rehabilitation planning.

Potential Study Impact

Suffolk County has a significant population of older adults (nearly 20% aged 65 and older) and

individuals with disabilities (approximately 8% under the age of 65) (U.S. Census Bureau, 2023).  These
barriers can impact social engagement, independence, and a sense of identity (Dickerson et al., 2024).
New York State Office For the Aging conducted a statewide needs assessment survey for older adults
from February 2023 through May 2023 (NYSOFA, 2025). This data identifies challenges that New York
drivers are experiencing and with limited support. Driving cessation is also a risk for older drivers and
drivers with disabilities and who often resist it due to the decreased activity engagement and
independence (Dickerson, 2024).

Driving rehabilitation programs do not currently implement smartphone based technology or

utilize passenger input for assessment and responsive intervention as naturalistic driving methods. Our
approach to explore these methods and contrast them with traditional tools positions our team at the
cutting edge of driving rehabilitation research. This approach also has great potential impact for clinical
care, introducing only nominal additional expenses (data storage, platform servicing) because of the
ubiquity of smartphones, and presents the opportunity to extend clinical services that potentially lead to
more effective driving rehabilitation outcomes. Furthermore, this project directly addresses a critical
service gap on Long Island, where a growing population of older adults and individuals with disabilities
face transportation barriers that affect independence and well-being. This project will build on a pilot
program taking place August 2025 and create a sustainable driving rehabilitation program by testing
real-world behavior monitoring. This project will:

●  Validate the mobile health app by comparing app-based measures of driving behavior (strategic,
tactical, operational) with established clinical assessments in vision, cognition, and motor
function (Aim 1)

●  Evaluate the feasibility and utility of a smartphone-based platform to collect real-world data on

driving behavior and self-reported fitness to drive among older adults (Aim 2)

●  Explore the relationship between clinical evaluation results and real-world driver and caregiver

feedback to support personalized, community-based driving interventions (Aim 3)
Overall, the pilot findings from our proposed SHP Seed Grant will support these innovative

research and scholarship activities that position the PI team for future foundation funding opportunities.
This project provides a foundation for publications, research collaborations, and interprofessional studies
related to driving rehabilitation. The pilot program’s data will be used to validate driving assessments and
intervention strategies, making the project highly competitive for national grants. The investigators plan
to apply for additional funding, including the American Occupational Therapy Foundation’s Intervention
Research and Implementation Research Grants (December 2025) the Mother Cabrini Health Foundation
(June 2026), and ASAHP’s Interprofessional Collaboration Research Grant (June 2026).

This project aligns with the SHP Research Seed Grant’s goals to support clinical advancement,
healthcare education, and technological innovation, and has strong potential to attract larger external
funding, including foundation grants (e.g., AOTF, ASAHP, Mother Cabrini Health Foundation). This
project will also have a strong interdisciplinary collaboration between the School of Health Professions’
occupational therapy program and Stony Brook Medicine’s Neurology Department. Both Drs. Ambrose
and Verghese have begun this collaboration with the PI team by exploring the SBM physician referral
system and recommend participants for this project by participating in refining the physician internal to
SBM referral procedures, as well as, screen and recommend patients for this study to Finally, the overall
program will reduce occupational disparities and inform the community and health care providers on a
community resource to support safe, independent mobility for aging and disabled populations.

Roles and responsibilities of PI and Co Investigators

The proposed study builds on the PI’s (Dr. Hannah Mercier) clinical research expertise in

technology-based methods to assess and promote health, mobility, and community participation among
adults with disabilities. Dr. Mercier will lead the study design, data analysis, and integration of the mobile
platform within the program. Acquiring SHP Seed Funding will strengthen her research portfolio and
position her as a competitive applicant for future federal and foundation grants in digital health and
rehabilitation.

The Driver Rehabilitation Program will be led by Dr. Rosalia Casanova, a licensed occupational
therapist, certified Driving Rehabilitation Professional (DRP), and certified Driving Instructor (CDI) in
New York State. The Co- PI will oversee the clinical aspects of the program, including protocol
development, administration of standardized assessments, and participant screening. Their unique
qualifications enable them to translate clinical evaluation findings into real-world driver
recommendations, bridging research and practice. Together, the PI and Co-PI bring complementary
strengths in clinical research, technology integration, and driver rehabilitation, ensuring the project is
methodologically sound, clinically relevant, and positioned for scalability through future external funding.
