A.  Personal Statement
I am an Assistant Professor of Occupational Therapy, and my research interests focus on enhancing health and
participation outcomes for adults with neurological conditions. My clinical background and postdoctoral research in
rehabilitation sciences created a strong interest in effects of neurological conditions and empowering self-management
approaches to mitigate them, specifically in the form of mobile health. I have collaborated with colleagues in computer
science, biomedical engineering, and psychiatry to develop these interventions, and have trained and supervised four
graduate computer science and mathematics students to specifically refine the mobile health technology and analytical
approaches to maximize clinical utility. The proposed project unites my research interests and skills to design and validate
an impactful, responsive, and personalized technology enhanced intervention to engage and support the driving
rehabilitation needs of adults with disabilities. Our team has developed this work through regular bimonthly meetings, and
each member brings exceptional complementary skills and experience. I am uniquely qualified to serve as the Principal
Investigator and lead the smartphone platform refinement, testing, and deployment, and study the use of mobile
technology for engaging driving rehabilitation participants. It is my hope that this work will enhance the assessment of
underlying driving skills with a more comprehensive and reliable reflection of performance and provide an accessible and
effective path for supporting driving participation among adults with disabilities.

C.  Contributions to Science
1. Doctoral Career: My research training has provided me with a strongly laid foundation in research with human subjects
and in exploiting large databases to explain the psychosocial components to activity and participation among people with
disabilities. As a doctoral fellow with Dr. Alan M. Jette at the New England Regional SCI Center (NERSCIC), I took
advantage of the wealth of the SCI Model Systems Database to better define the relationship of hospitalization with
subsequent outcomes of disability and psychosocial function. I received the University of Alabama Birmingham Devivo
Mentored Research award to complete this work, and I provided new evidence identifying characteristics of those at
greatest risk for poor outcomes following hospitalization. I presented findings at several conferences, and authored
publications that composed part of my doctoral dissertation. At NERSCIC I led another project to evaluate the efficacy of a
responsive telehealth intervention to decrease the severity of depression among adults with SCI. I found that those who
were depressed initially demonstrated significant improvements in depressive symptoms and tended to have fewer
hospitalizations after the treatment. This retrospective analysis also revealed that, in comparison to a population with
similar functional limitations, those with SCI were less engaged in the intervention and in turn required more extensive
follow-up and individualized support. My final actions at NERSCIC were to develop a peer-led telehealth intervention
applying an empowerment model to more effectively engage those with SCI to successfully self-manage their health.

A. Personal Statement
I am a licensed occupational therapist, certified Driver Rehabilitation Professional (DRP), and New York
State certified driving instructor, with advanced clinical training in adult physical rehabilitation. I am
currently the only DRP on Long Island with a healthcare background who provides both comprehensive
clinical and behind-the-wheel evaluations. My professional expertise enables me to bridge the gap
between medical evaluation, driver instruction, and rehabilitation research. This SHP SEED grant
supports the next phase of a driver rehabilitation initiative that builds upon a prior pilot study. The initial
pilot focuses on designing a mobile health application informed by Michon’s model of driving behavior,
with emphasis on observable traffic-level driving behaviors. This process also included mentoring an
undergraduate research student who was selected and awarded a $5,000 scholarship for research
studies to collaborate on this pilot study with the PI team. In this next phase, we aim to evaluate how data
from the app—gathered by passenger observers (e.g., caregivers or family members)—correlates with
outcomes from formal clinical driving evaluations and self-performance ratings. The goal is to create an
innovative resource tool for drivers caregivers, providers, and driver rehabilitation professionals to better
understand and monitor an individual’s driving performance. This project is innovative in that it introduces
a mobile, community-based dyadic model where observers contribute real-time behavioral data,
supporting a more holistic approach to driver safety and clinical decision-making. I bring critical expertise
in evaluating medical fitness to drive, including interpreting motor, visual, and cognitive functioning. I will
serve as the clinical lead in this study—overseeing participant evaluations, supervising app deployment,
and analyzing relationships between app, clinical, and self-assessment data. My prior work in geriatric fall
prevention, culturally responsive clinical education, and simulation-based DEI training reflects a broader
commitment to advancing equitable, accessible, and evidence-based practices across rehabilitation
contexts.
