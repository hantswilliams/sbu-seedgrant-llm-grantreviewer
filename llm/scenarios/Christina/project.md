**IMPACT-FP: Interprofessional Model for Prevention and Clinical Training Falls Prevention Program: Empowering older adults and future clinicians through a university-community partnership - A pilot study**

**Project Description**

**Background & Problem Statement**

Falls are the leading cause of both fatal and non-fatal injuries among older adults, resulting in decreased independence, increased healthcare utilization, and significant psychosocial impacts (CDC, 2023). One in four Americans aged 65+ falls each year, and of those, 20% suffer serious injuries such as fractures or head trauma (Burns & Kakara, 2018). Evidence-based interventions, including balance training and strength exercises, can substantially reduce the incidence of falls. However, many older adults do not engage in such programs due to a lack of access or awareness.

Additionally, interprofessional collaboration among healthcare providers—particularly physical therapists (PT) and occupational therapists (OT)—supports multimodal approaches known to optimize fall risk reduction. Even so, few opportunities exist within academic curricula to immerse students in real-world, interprofessional collaboration with direct patient care responsibility. This pilot study addresses both of these critical gaps within our study objectives: (1) decrease falls and fall risk in community-dwelling older adults through a structured, multifactorial falls prevention workshop, and (2) enhance the interprofessional education and collaborative skills of PT and OT students through a supervised, team-based clinical experience.

**Specific Aims**

1. Evaluate the preliminary impact of an 8-week interprofessional falls prevention program on multifactorial fall risk among community-dwelling older adults.
2. Examine the program's acceptability, engagement, and perceived saliency and difficulty through weekly feedback from older adult participants.
3. Enhance interprofessional collaboration and learning outcomes across rehabilitation student leaders/participants.

**Methodical Approach and Timeline**

The groundwork for this study has been established over the previous two years through regular team meetings and outreach to align the vision with SHP priorities, student input, departmental support, and the Strategic Initiatives of Stony Brook Southampton. As a result, Stony Brook University Institutional Review Board has approved this work, the majority of tools and equipment have been procured, SHP Dean Jaffee Gropack has granted use of the space for this program, and Vice President Wendy Pearson has pledged support.

**Study Design:** This is a mixed-methods pilot study involving pre and post-assessments for both older adult participants and student leaders. We will implement an 8-week interprofessional Falls Prevention intervention for community-dwelling older adults in a high-need, under-resourced community, delivered by PT/OT students under direct faculty supervision. To address **Aims 1 & 2, focused on the older adult participants**, older adults will be recruited from the local East End community through existing partnerships with community-based organizations, senior centers, libraries, and utilizing the Stony Brook Southampton Hospital’s existing wellness e-blast contact lists. Up to 25 individuals will be screened for appropriateness of participation according to the Centers for Disease Control's STEADI fall risk algorithm (CDC, 2019). If they are determined to be too unsteady to participate, they will be referred to appropriate clinical resources and rehabilitative care. A cohort of 10-12 older adults will be enrolled in the intervention. Participant perspectives on the program and any change in pre-post scores will guide refinement and expansion of the program.

Thepre/post intervention evaluation battery will include the following metrics, administered by student leaders and supervised by trained faculty:

* Activities-Based Confidence Scale (ABC scale)
* Short Falls Efficacy Scale I (Short-FES I)
* Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS)
* Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA)
* Timed Up and Go (TUG)
* 30-Second Chair Stand Test
* 4-stage balance test
* 10-meter walk test (Gait Velocity)

On a weekly basis, participants will report the context of any falls or near-falls they experienced during the preceding week, along with a satisfaction survey post-session.

**Intervention Description:** The SAFE program intervention will consist of 8 weekly, 120-minute sessions delivered on Stony Brook University Southampton campus. Each session will include:

* 50 minutes of educational content (fall risk, home safety, vision, medications, etc.)
* 50 minutes of structured, progressive balance and strength training exercises
* 2 10-minute breaks following each session for socialization and feedback

**To study Aim 3, focused on the student leaders,** 15-20 second and third-year Stony Brook University PT and OT graduate students will be selected to participate. Faculty from both disciplines will supervise all sessions. Student leaders will be familiar with the assessments and educational content and will have planned sessions that are reviewed with faculty. Prior to and following their participation, students will complete the Students Perceptions of Interprofessional Clinical Education Revised (SPICE-R) to measure attitudes toward interprofessional teams and the team approach to care. At the conclusion of their participation as Student leaders, they will complete a short survey and respond to open-ended questions pertaining to their performance and learning within the interprofessional setting.

**Potential Study Impact**

This pilot addresses a critical public health concern—falls in older adults—through a sustainable, educational-community partnership model. If successful, this model can be scaled and embedded into clinical education curricula, offering dual benefits:

1. **Improved community health outcomes** through fall risk reduction and enhanced physical function.
2. **Enhanced student training** in interprofessional collaboration, preparing graduates to deliver team-based care.

The findings will inform a larger, multi-site grant application and support the case for interprofessional, student-led clinical models within allied health education. The program could expand to include students from other programs within the SHP, such as Physician Assistant Studies, Respiratory Care, and Speech-Language Pathology. Exit survey information from both older adult participants and student leaders will identify this need for a more integrated, collaborative approach and the desired team composition. For example, the strong health professions programs at Stony Brook University afford the opportunity to foster even greater interprofessional learning and holistic care through engaging students from outside SHP, including Nursing, Social Work, and Medicine. With the successful implementation of this pilot, the program could be expanded to additional sites, including the Stony Brook Main Campus, to increase community access and student involvement. Furthermore, this study collaborates with Stony Brook Medicine Southampton in outreach efforts to meet the needs of older adults on Eastern Long Island. While this initially casts a broad net to older adults with fall risk, it presents the opportunity to complement existing Stony Brook Medicine programming specific to at-risk populations, such as Parkinson’s Disease.
