i. Background & Problem Statement:

LD (LD) is caused by the spirochete bacteria Borrelia burgdorferi and is the most common
tick-borne disease in the United States.1 The disease is typically transmitted by a blacklegged
tick (Ixodes scapularis) through a bite delivered to the host.2 According to the Center for
Disease Control (CDC), the number of individuals diagnosed with, and treated for,  LD annually
is estimated to approach approximately 476,000 cases.3  Interestingly, only 89,000 cases were
officially reported to the CDC in 2023, meaning that the estimates include a probable ~400,000
cases being undiagnosed or misdiagnosed annually.3 Treatment typically consists of oral
antibiotic therapy, however, intravenous antibiotics may be prescribed.4 Unfortunately, despite
treatment, an estimated 10% of cases proceed to Post-Treatment LD Syndrome (PTLDS),
which is a chronic condition featuring the hallmark symptoms of fatigue, sleep disturbances,
pain, and cognitive impairments.5

Medical schools have benefitted from the discussions and research efforts in response to the LD
epidemic, including expanded education, diagnostics, and treatment pathways.6,7 However,
medical doctors are no longer the first point of contact for patients presenting with LD
symptoms. Physical therapists (PT) and are providers who will frequently encounter patients
presenting with LD symptoms before, or in lieu of, a medical doctor. Therefore, it is of critical
importance that they be able differentially diagnose LD from other common conditions that can
imitate the infection.  For example, a swollen, stiff, and painful knee joint could be osteoarthritis
or a joint sprain, but it also could be the manifestations of an untreated LD infection.8 As direct
access providers of choice for musculoskeletal issues, it becomes paramount that a PT
recognize these sometimes vague and transient symptoms as potential LD so a referral can be
made for medical examination and treatment.

While medical schools have made progress, it is unclear if the PT profession has made the
necessary curricular revisions to address their increased role in differentially diagnosing LD.
Since we know LD is frequently under or misdiagnosed, and we know that PT’s are first line
providers, understanding the efforts being undertaken in the academic programs licensing these
practitioners regarding LD and diagnosis is key. Additionally, there are no studies that currently
examine a practicing PT’s ability to differentially diagnose LD, and only one study specifically
published on LD diagnosis and PT.9  Therefore, the purpose of this study is to survey educators
about the curricular trends surrounding LD in PT programs, as well as survey clinicians to
assess their ability to differentially diagnose LD.  By identifying these factors, we can better
advocate for the need for further educational interventions and better prepare future PT’s for
clinic practice.

ii. Specific Aims

There are two Aims of this study.  Aim 1 is to explore curricular trends related to LD in
accredited PT programs.  Aim 2 is to assess differential diagnostic capabilities as it relates to LD
among practicing PT’s.

iii. Methodical Approach and Timeline

For Aim 1, a survey will be designed and content validated specific to the curricular trends in PT
programs related to LD. The inclusion criteria for participating in the survey is that: 1) they are a
faculty member or instructor in an accredited/candidate PT program, and 2) have knowledge of
the curricular trends involving LD in their program. Recruitment will occur in three ways:  posting
to the Education Special Interest Group of the Academy of Education discussion board, direct
email to the Chair/Program Director of all PT programs, and in-person data collection at the
Combined Sections Meeting (CSM) in February of 2026. The study will continue until 100
participants are reached (the limit of requested funds and a sample of ~33% of all PT programs)
before the survey will be closed. Only one participant from each program can complete the
survey (a question on the survey will ask which program they are representing and the
institution will be cross-referenced to prevent duplicates). In addition to collecting a demographic
profile on each participant, the main themes to be explored are the amount of time in the
program dedicated to LD education, the modes in which the education occurs, and degree of
focus on differential diagnosis. The survey will be created by the research team, then content
validated by at least two individuals unrelated to the study but who have expertise in the content
area and survey design. The survey will be piloted prior to being deployed. Data will be
analyzed using descriptive statistics and crosstabulation for the variable of geographic location.
It is hypothesized that geographical location may influence the amount of LD education in a
program due to the disease’s preponderance to the particular locales. A $10 gift card will be
digitally sent to participants within 4 weeks of receiving a completed survey.

Aim 2 will be a combination of a survey and questionnaire. Inclusion criteria for the second Aim
is that they are a licensed physical therapist practicing in an outpatient setting. They are
excluded from participating in the study if they are faculty members in a PT program (to avoid
possible identification of the study’s purpose and redundancy among the samples). Recruitment
will occur in the following ways: posting on social media, posting on the American Physical
Therapy Association discussion boards, and in-person data collection at CSM in February of
2026.  The study will continue until 100 participants are reached (the limit of requested funds)
and the survey will be closed. The survey will collect demographic data and define the
characteristics of the participating physical therapists. The questionnaire portion will be a series
of case scenarios presenting a simulated patient encounter. The scenarios will require the
participant to determine the differential diagnosis by listing (in order of likelihood) the top 3
diagnoses they believe to be presented by the scenario. They will then be asked to choose their
primary diagnosis and why.  The survey and questionnaire will not mention LD as this would
disclose the purpose of the study and influence results. The survey and questionnaire for Aim 2
will be content validated by experts in test writing and piloted before deployment.  Data analysis
will explore the demographics and answers to the questionnaire using descriptive statistics, and
compare the responses for accuracy based on geographical location and setting.  It is
hypothesized that there will be a difference in the diagnosis of LD based on the region of the
participant completing the questionnaire. A $20 gift card will be digitally sent to participants
within 2 weeks of receiving a completed survey. The increased reimbursement from Aim 1 is
due to the estimated increase in time required to complete the study.

iv. Potential Study Impact

Each aim individually addresses important concerns affecting PT practice.  The results of Aim 1
can be used as a baseline to draw comparisons across the hundreds of different PT programs
nationwide.  Programs can benchmark themselves against the results of the study in order to
address shortfalls in their curriculum. It may also serve as a repository of potential teaching
methodologies for educators to utilize in their own programs.

The results of Aim 2 will address the question of a PT’s ability to differentially diagnose LD from
other common imitators, shedding light on the degree of underdiagnosis present among
clinicians. This knowledge will bring to light gaps in clinical practice, allowing for discussion and
advocacy efforts to have a basis in evidence.

Taking the two Aims together, a strong case for the need to reform PT education and practice
can be made. If the studies correctly identify that there is a paucity of education on LD
differential diagnosis, and that we have evidence of misdiagnosis clinically, then the next logical
step is to address the issue through educational interventions directed at both PT students and
practicing clinicians.

Furthermore, future studies could expand the scope of practice beyond physical therapists and
examine the curricular trends and diagnostic capabilities in other fields, including but not limited
to physician assistants and nurse practitioners.

vi. Plans for Future Grant Submissions and Dissemination

Each of the aims are individual scholarly products consisting of a platform or poster presentation
as well as a manuscript (a total of 4 scholarly products).  The results of Aim 1 would be
submitted for presentation at the Educator Leadership Conference (ELC), as well being
submitted for publication in the Journal of Physical Therapy Education (JOPTE). The second
Aim would be submitted for presentation at the Combined Sections Meeting (CSM) of the
American Physical Therapy Association (APTA) and submitted for publication in the Journal of
Physical Therapy (PTJ).

The combined results of the study are expected to indicate that there is both a lack of time spent
addressing LD diagnosis in physical therapy education as well as a failure of practicing physical
therapists to correctly identify LD when presented with case scenarios. The results of the study
can be used to support the need for an interventional study aimed at physical therapist
education and clinical practice. The international Lyme and Associated Diseases Educational
Foundation (ILADEF) has an annual grant specifically designed to fund education related to LD
(https://iladef.org/grants-available/).  This includes the development and distribution of
educational materials on tick borne diseases and other related dissemination costs.
