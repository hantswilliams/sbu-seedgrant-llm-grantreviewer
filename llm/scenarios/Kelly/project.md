Integrating  Physical  Activity  and  VR-Based  Mindfulness  to  Support  Stress  and
Cardiometabolic Health in Breast Cancer Patients: A Feasibility Study
Research  Team:  Kelly  Warren,  PhD,  Sharon  Martino  PT,  PhD,  Jules  A.  Cohen,  MD,
Adam Gonzalez, PhD

Background  &  Problem  Statement:  Breast  cancer (BC) is one of the most prevalent
cancers  among  women  (American  Cancer  Society  2025)  Even  in  survivorship,
psychological  distress  (Tang  et al. 2024), physical discomfort, and cardiometabolic risk
(Jung  et  al.  2025)  are  common.  Clinically  significant  psychological  symptoms  are
reported in up to 50% of patients with BC during the stages of diagnosis, treatment, and
rehabilitation,  seriously  impacting  quality  of  life (QOL). Depression, anxiety, and stress
symptoms  are  particularly  high  in  this  patient  population  (Zhang  et  al.  2022)  and
comprehensive care models are proposed to not only support patient outcomes through
medical  intervention  but  also  by  boosting  access  to  psychological  support and mental
health  resources  (Singer  et  al.  2010).   The  physical  symptom  burden  in  patients  with
breast  cancer  is  equally  problematic.  Patients  evaluated  by the Distress Thermometer
and  Problem  List  (DT&PL)  report  high  levels  of  fatigue,  sleep  disturbance,  pain,
neurological compromise, as well as other organ and systemic impact (McFarland et al.
2018).  These  outcomes  are  often  consequences  of  interventional  treatments  like
surgery,  radiation,  chemotherapy,  and  hormone  modifying  pharmacologics,  and   they
strongly  correlate  to  psychological  distress  and  QOL  scores  (McFarland  et  al.  2018).
Medical  interventions  for  breast  cancer  also  impact  cardiometabolic  health,  including
higher risks of hypertension and type 2 diabetes. Among women with early stage breast
cancer,  cardiometabolic  risk  factors  were  significantly  associated  with  an  increased
development  of  heart  failure  (Mavrogeni  et  al.  2019)  and  with  cardiovascular  and
all-cause  mortality  (Simon  et  al.  2018).  Finally,

Specific Aims:
Aim  1:  Assess  the  feasibility  of  implementing  a  12-week  combined  PA  (with  FitBit
trackers)  and  VR-based  mindfulness  intervention  in  patients  diagnosed  with  BC.
Feasibility  will  be  evaluated  through  recruitment  and  retention  rates,  adherence,  and
participant satisfaction.
Aim  2:  Evaluate  preliminary  effects  of  the  intervention  on  perceived  stress  and
cardiometabolic  health.  Outcomes  will  include  changes  in  biomarkers  (e.g.,  cortisol,
C-reactive  protein  [CRP]),  self-reported  stress,  and  cardiometabolic  indicators  (e.g.,
blood pressure, RHR, BMI).
Hypotheses:  H1:  the  combined  intervention  will  be  feasible  and  acceptable  to
participants.  H2:  the  combined  intervention  will  lead  to  improvements  in  perceived
stress and cardiometabolic health compared to baseline measures.

incentivized by providing participants a FitBit Inspire 3 and exercise equipment package
at  the  start  of  the  intervention,  as  well  as  a  gift  card  for  data  collection  completion.
Interventions:  Participants  will  receive  a  patient-centered  PA  and  VR  mindfulness
intervention  along  with  a  wearable  tracking  device  (FitBit:  Inspire  3)  to  monitor  and
record key health metrics. In addition to the home exercise component, participants will
be  provided  with  a  VR  unit  and  be  asked  to  complete  a  minimum  of  three  20-minute
mindfulness sessions per week for 12 weeks using the VR headset. The headset will be
returned  following  the  study.  Prior  to  enrollment,  all  participants  will  undergo  informed
consent  process  in  accordance  with  IRB  guidelines.  They  will  also  receive training on
the  use  of the FitBit, VR hardware, and receive exercise-related support through study

personnel. Data Collection: All study measurements will be performed at two timepoints:
T01= baseline and T02 = following the 12-week intervention period. The goal will be to
align the T01 and T02 timepoints with the start and end, respectively, of each subject’s
chemotherapy  regime.  All  measurements  will  be  conducted  in  the  Rehabilitation
Research and Movement Performance lab.
Primary Outcomes:
1)  Vital  signs/anthropometrics:  Resting  heart  rate,  blood  pressure,  height,  and  weight

will be collected. Height and weight will be used to compute BMI (kg/m2).

2)  Body composition: Percent body fat will be measured via air plethysmography (ADP)
using  a  Bod  Pod™  and  3D  scanning  (Styku™).  For  body  composition  (and  blood
work), participants will be asked to fast for 8-12 hours prior to the exam and to refrain
from exercise for one day prior to the exam.

3)  Cardiorespiratory  endurance:  The  6-minute walk test and the three-minute step test

will be used to test heart rate recovery and  cardiovascular endurance.

5)  Cardiometabolic  blood  outcomes:  Whole  blood  will  be collected via venipuncture at
the  start  of  the  study  (baseline,  T01)  and  following  the  10  week  study  intervention
(T02).  Blood  panels  will  include:  total  blood  count  (RBC,  WBC,  and  platelets),  full
lipid panel (total cholesterol, low and high density lipoproteins and triglycerides (LDL,
HDL, and TGs), cortisol, and CRP.

6)  Feasibility:  will  be  evaluated  through  recruitment  and  retention  rates,  adherence  to
the  intervention  (#  of  sessions  and  amount  of  physical  activity  and  mindfulness
exercises completed), and participant feedback via journaling.

Statistics:  Analyses  will  be  conducted  using  SSPS  v29.  Descriptive  statistics  will  be
completed to evaluate both the T01 and T02 outcomes. Paired t-tests (using SNK post
hoc)  will  be  run  to  determine  if  significant  differences  (P<0.05)  exist  in  primary
outcomes. It is anticipated that statistical power will be low given the low sample size for
this  feasibility  study,  but  variability  and  effect  size  in  data  will  guide  sample  size
determination for a larger RCT study design.
Project Timeline:

Potential Study Impact: The long-term goal of this project is to develop an integrated,
scalable  and  patient-centered  intervention  using  technology  such  as  PA  trackers  and
VR units, that can enhance quality of life and long-term health outcomes among breast
cancer  survivors—and  potentially  extend  to  individuals  surviving  other  cancer  types.
This  aim  is  closely  aligned  with  the  NIH  mission  to  support  research  that  improves
health, prolongs life, and reduces the burden of illness and disability.

provides expertise in the assessment and interpretation of change to medical, physical,
and  mental  health  outcomes  in  the  subject  population.  Kelly  Warren  (physiologist,
School  of  Health  Professions)  will  serve  as  PI  and  be responsible for study oversight,
including submission of IRB and future grant applications. She will organize the team to
execute the 12-week intervention, and lead data collection, analysis, and interpretation.
Sharon  Martino  (physical  therapist,  School  of  Health  Professions)  will  guide  the
12-week  exercise  program  and  participate  in  data  collection  of  anthropometrics  and

recruit  and  screen  participants,  order  and  interpret  blood  tests  and  results,  as  well as
