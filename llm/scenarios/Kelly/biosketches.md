A. Personal Statement
I am excited to have put together an interdisciplinary team to explore outcomes of a physical exercise
and virtual-reality mindfulness intervention in breast cancer patients. My education and research
interests are within the biomedical sciences. I have demonstrated research productivity across
physiologic disciplines including participation in studies evaluating respiratory neural control,
neuroendocrinology of reproduction, and energy requirements during exercise. More recently, I serve
as principal investigator for a study evaluating student health and wellness in first year graduate allied
health students. I am also co-investigator on a project exploring physical fatigue in a population of
patients with Lyme disease and participated on a service-learning project for health professional
students to engage on interdisciplinary teams in an exercise-related intervention for children who are
overweight or obese. For both activities, I have participated in data collection (e.g. submaximal
exercise testing) in line with my role on the current proposed project. Additionally, my scholarly activity
has provided me strengths across various research designs and methodologies, and I have
experiences that include biomolecular bench work, in vivo and in vitro animal studies, and human
subject research. In each of these research disciplines, I have demonstrated scholarly productivity
through peer-reviewed research publications and presentations. I am eager to lead this collaborative
team created to evaluate the role of wearable devices and mindfulness on breast cancer recovery,
eventually contributing data to these measures in predicting comorbidities and extending to other
populations.

as  well  as  other  animal  studies  has  influenced  my  work  on  human  subjects  looking  at  aerobic
capacity in patients who are struggling with fatigue due to Lyme Disease.
